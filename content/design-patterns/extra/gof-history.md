# The Gang of Four & Origins of Design Patterns

Software design patterns did not start in computer science—they began in **building architecture**.

---

## 1. Christopher Alexander's Pattern Language (1977)

In 1977, architect **Christopher Alexander** published *A Pattern Language*, cataloging 253 recurring architectural solutions for towns, buildings, and rooms (such as *Light on Two Sides of Every Room*). His core insight was that good design is not invented from scratch every time; it is composed from a shared language of proven, human-centric solutions.

---

## 2. From Buildings to Smalltalk (1987)

A decade later, **Ward Cunningham** and **Kent Beck** adapted Alexander's ideas to software while building Smalltalk user interfaces. At OOPSLA 1987, they presented five UI patterns—introducing the software community to the idea of naming recurring object-oriented structures.

---

## 3. The Gang of Four Book (1994)

Between 1990 and 1994, **Erich Gamma**, **Richard Helm**, **Ralph Johnson**, and **John Vlissides** (nicknamed the **Gang of Four** or **GoF**) compiled 23 battle-tested patterns observed across C++ and Smalltalk frameworks into *Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software*.

Why the book reshaped software engineering:

- **Shared Vocabulary:** Engineers could say "wrap this with a Decorator" or "extract a Strategy" instead of drawing twenty classes on a whiteboard.
- **Three-Category Taxonomy:** Grouping patterns into **Creational**, **Structural**, and **Behavioral** gave developers a mental index for diagnosing design problems.
- **Composition Over Inheritance:** It popularized runtime composition as the primary tool for flexible object-oriented systems.
